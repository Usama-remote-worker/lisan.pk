**“Under Process” on the Study in Saudi portal means your application is still being reviewed; it is not an acceptance or rejection.** A university reviews a submitted scholarship application first, and an approved application then passes through further university and Saudi Ministry of Education procedures. **There is no single official result date for all applicants**, so your own portal status and instructions are more reliable than social-media predictions. [1]

**2026 intake update (last checked 5 November 2026):** The Study in Saudi portal displayed **16 September–6 October 2026** as the application window for that intake. That submission period has ended. The portal can still be used to follow existing applications, but do not confuse an old application window with a verified date for the next intake. [2]

**Check your result directly:** [Official Study in Saudi website](https://studyinsaudi.sa/en) → sign in → open your applications or requests → select the relevant application. The exact wording and action buttons in your own account take priority over the general descriptions below.

> **Quick answer:** **Submitted** = sent for review; **Under Process** = being reviewed; **Returned** = inspect what the university wants corrected; **Nominated / Preliminary Approval** = a possible intermediate selection or approval stage, not proof of final admission; **Accepted / Final Approval** = check the stage and formal next action; **Rejected** = the application was not accepted. The official FAQ explicitly defines *Under Process* and outlines the university-to-ministry workflow; other labels should be interpreted using the actual portal notice. [1]

## Study in Saudi status meanings at a glance

| Status or message | Practical meaning | What it does **not** prove | What you should do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Draft / Incomplete** | Application not fully submitted, if shown in your account | That you applied successfully | Complete only when a relevant program is open and the portal permits submission. |
| **Submitted** | Application sent into the review workflow | Admission, scholarship or visa approval | Save your application number; monitor messages. |
| **Under Process / Under Review / تحت الإجراء** | The relevant educational institution is processing your case; this wording is explained in the official FAQ. [1] | That you have been rejected or selected | Check the application details and wait unless action is requested. |
| **Returned / Sent Back** | Application requires review of the portal's return notice | Automatic rejection or permission to change every field | Read the exact notice, correct only permitted items, and follow the displayed resubmission option. |
| **Nominated / Recommended** | An intermediate recommendation or selection label, where used by a university or your account | Final ministry approval or a guaranteed scholarship | Check which body nominated you; await the next official decision. |
| **Preliminary Acceptance** | An intermediate approval stage, where displayed | Permission to travel or issue a visa | Follow the next official request; await final acceptance/visa instructions. |
| **Accepted / Approved** | An approval at the particular stage shown in your record | Always a final scholarship award, visa or travel authorisation | Open the full decision, confirm whether it is final, and follow the university/portal instructions. |
| **Complete Visa Application / Visa Information** | Your account is asking for visa information as part of the post-admission process | That the visa has been issued | Complete the official portal form only when this action is enabled for your case. |
| **Rejected / Not Accepted** | The application did not meet, or was not approved under, the relevant review requirements [1] | That every future Saudi application is barred | Read the stated reason, check eligibility and future application opportunities. |

**Important:** These are reader-friendly explanations. The portal can display different text depending on the university, language and stage. **“Nominated” is not a universal final scholarship status defined on the public Study in Saudi FAQ**; never treat it as a guaranteed result without an official final decision.

## How to check your Study in Saudi application status

**Log in to the official portal and open the specific submitted application—not just your profile dashboard.** Applications may carry distinct statuses, messages, or requested actions.

1. Go to [studyinsaudi.sa](https://studyinsaudi.sa/en) and open the official **Sign In** page.
2. Enter your own registered login details. If you forgot your password, use the portal's **Recover Password** function. [1]
3. Open **My Services** and the section labelled **My Applications / My Requests**, or the corresponding menu shown in your version of the site.
4. Select the relevant application number and university/program.
5. Read the **status**, decision notes, any outstanding requests and messages.
6. Save a private record of the status and date. If support is needed, record your application reference but **never share your password or OTP**.

If a menu label changes, use the navigation actually shown in your account. LISAN.PK does not operate the official government portal and cannot open or amend an applicant's Ministry record.

## What does “Under Process” mean in Study in Saudi?

**It means the application is under consideration by the relevant educational institution.** The official portal asks applicants to keep monitoring until the process reaches an acceptance or rejection decision. [1]

The publicly described workflow is:

**Application submitted → university reviews it → university approval, if granted → institution and Ministry of Education complete later admission procedures → final decision and applicable next actions.** [1]

An Under Process result is therefore **not a rejection**. It is also **not proof you will receive a scholarship**. The status alone does not reveal your ranking, chance of selection or exactly when a decision will appear.

### Does Under Process mean I passed the first screening?

**Not necessarily.** It confirms processing, not completion of a specific screening stage unless the portal clearly states that the stage has been passed.

### What if my status has stayed Under Process for weeks?

**The official FAQ does not publish one maximum review time that applies to all universities and programs.** Check the application details for a new note or deadline, review official university communication, and keep monitoring. If the portal displays a technical error or a specific action deadline, use official support rather than guessing that a delayed result equals rejection. [1]

### Can I cancel an Under Process application?

**Only in a limited situation.** The official FAQ says cancellation may be available when the educational institution has **not yet taken action** on the request. Check whether your account provides the option; do not assume every Under Process application can be withdrawn. [1]

## What does “Nominated” mean on Study in Saudi?

**A nomination is not the same thing as a final scholarship acceptance.** Where a university, faculty, application screen or email uses *nominated*, *recommended* or similar wording, it commonly indicates selection or recommendation at a particular stage. **The public Study in Saudi FAQ does not provide one universal, authoritative definition of “Nominated” that applies to every program.**

Treat nomination as an **intermediate-status interpretation** until you can answer three questions:

1. **Who nominated you?** A department, university admissions unit or scholarship body?
2. **What exactly does the notice say?** Does it say preliminary, conditional or final?
3. **What action is requested next?** Additional documents, Ministry processing, an acceptance response or visa information?

The official FAQ confirms that **university approval and subsequent Ministry/institution procedures are separate parts of the process**. It is therefore unsafe to conclude that nomination means a visa is ready, a ticket has been booked or admission cannot be withdrawn. [1]

**Practical rule:** Keep the notification, follow official instructions, and wait for the explicit final admission/visa step instead of acting on social-media claims that a nomination guarantees departure.

## Preliminary acceptance vs final acceptance: what changes?

**Preliminary acceptance indicates progress; final acceptance is the stage at which the institution or portal confirms the decision and directs the applicant to applicable admission and visa steps.** The exact labels depend on the account and program. University approval does not automatically mean that every Ministry procedure is complete. [1]

| Question | Preliminary / university-level approval | Confirmed final decision |
|---|---|---|
| Has the university shown a positive step? | Possibly | Yes, if the decision is final as stated |
| Is Ministry processing necessarily complete? | **No** | Confirm the exact final notice and remaining conditions |
| Can I book a flight? | **Not based on this label** | Only when required travel documents and university instructions are complete |
| Should I submit educational-visa data? | Only if specifically requested | Follow the action displayed by the official account |
| Is an acceptance letter always available immediately? | No | Check the official downloads/notices; availability varies |

Do not use the words *accepted*, *approved*, *nominated* and *visa issued* interchangeably.

## Study in Saudi acceptance letter: how to confirm it is genuine

**A genuine acceptance document should come through an official university or portal channel and match your own application.** Verify your name, program, university, intake, conditions, application reference and any follow-up requirements.

Check the following before assuming the letter is enough for visa or travel arrangements:

- Is the document **preliminary, conditional or final**?
- Is the issuing university correctly named and linked to your submitted application?
- Are your name, nationality and passport details consistent?
- Are translation, attestation, medical or other document requirements listed?
- Does it explicitly direct you to a visa-related action, or is further approval pending?

**Do not pay a third party for a purported Ministry approval letter.** If you receive a letter outside the official account, verify it with the issuing university through its published channels.

## Study in Saudi application “Returned”: what should you do?

**Returned means you should read the university/portal notice before taking action; it is not automatically the same as Rejected.** The FAQ separately discusses editing an application returned by a university. [1]

### Five-step returned-application checklist

1. **Open the returned application**, not just the summary status.
2. **Read the reason or reviewer comment.** Identify the exact field or file in question; do not assume every return concerns document translation.
3. **Check what can be edited.** Only use fields and actions visibly enabled in the portal for your specific record.
4. **Correct the requested issue accurately.** Where a document is unclear, upload a more readable, authentic file only if the system invites a replacement. Do not change names or dates to fit an expected result.
5. **Resubmit or confirm the correction** only as the account instructs. Save the new submission status and any acknowledgement.

If the university requests a better academic certificate, transcript or translation, check the receiving institution's requirements before paying for a new version. LISAN.PK can help translate or review documents, **not override the portal's locks or reverse official decisions**.

### Can I edit my passport number after submission?

**The official Study in Saudi FAQ says a passport number cannot ordinarily be changed after an application has been submitted using it.** The FAQ specifically says a passport number can be updated when no applications have yet been submitted; it cannot be modified after submission through the regular process. [1]

Keep evidence of both old and renewed passports where relevant, and contact the official help route if your situation needs clarification. **Do not create a second account with inaccurate identity information to bypass a restriction.**

### Can I update my qualifications or contact details?

**The official site currently contains two differently worded answers.** Its homepage FAQ says contact information is editable while other submitted application data is not. The detailed FAQ says qualification data and certain specified fields may be updated, but not passport/Gulf ID or residency number, gender or nationality. [1–2]

This means it is **not safe to publish a blanket claim** that all qualifications can always be edited—or that none can. **Your actual editable fields, application status and official support instructions control.** If a correction is essential and the field is locked, use the [official help section](https://studyinsaudi.sa/en/Faq) rather than attempting a workaround.

### Can I change the email registered with Study in Saudi?

The FAQ distinguishes between account types: a registrant who used an email address generally cannot change that registered email, while certain residents in Saudi Arabia may have an email-update option through the profile. Check the official answer for your account category. [1]

## What if Study in Saudi says “Rejected”?

**Rejected means the application was not approved.** The official FAQ explains that a rejection can occur when one or more requirements of the program or educational institution have not been met. It does **not** state that every rejection has the same cause. [1]

The sensible next steps are to:

1. Read the complete decision and any reason displayed.
2. Compare your academic record, documents and eligibility with the **specific university/program criteria**.
3. Correct genuine document issues for a future eligible intake, if applicable.
4. Check whether the university provides a formal review route; **do not assume there is an appeal button**.
5. Explore other verified programs rather than immediately submitting duplicate applications or trusting “guaranteed selection” sellers.

A rejection is **not evidence that you can buy a reconsideration, pay to change the result or receive admission by translating unrelated documents**.
