## English-to-Arabic translation for documents—not machine-generated wording

An English-to-Arabic document translation must preserve more than the rough meaning of each sentence. Names, dates, qualifications, legal obligations, numerical amounts, institutions and supporting notes all matter. A small error in a degree title, marriage record or company document can create inconsistencies when the translation is compared with the original.

For document requests, LISAN.PK focuses on the **purpose of use**, the **complete source text** and the **required submission format**. ## English-to-Arabic translation for Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia is a common destination for educational, employment, family and business documents. However, there is **no single translation rule for every Saudi visa or university**.

### University admission or scholarship

Check the exact university/program checklist. If it requires Arabic translations of English academic documents, provide the specified records and any instructions on certification or official authentication. Do not assume that Arabic translation is compulsory merely because the university is in Saudi Arabia.

### Employment and professional matters

An employer or regulator may ask for Arabic versions of degrees, experience letters, professional licences or company-issued certificates. Requirements differ by occupation and receiving organisation. A regulated profession may require additional recognition or licensing independent of translation.

### Family or civil documents

If the original source is a Pakistani **English-language Marriage Registration Certificate (MRC)**, the requested Arabic translation may be different from a full Urdu **Nikah Nama** contract translation. Ask the receiving authority which document it expects. **[Nikah Nama and MRC translation services](/services/translation/nikah-nama-marriage-certificate)**.

### Authentication and apostille are separate

Pakistan's MOFA currently lists Saudi Arabia among apostille-accepting destinations for eligible documents. Nonetheless, document-category rules and the receiving organisation's explicit requirements still matter. **Translation is not a substitute for an apostille**, and an apostille on an original does not automatically authenticate a separate translation.

## English-to-Arabic translation for the UAE, Qatar and Gulf organisations

Gulf destinations do **not** share one universal translation-and-attestation system. For example, the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs publishes a separate official digital document-attestation route for Pakistan-issued records. That process can involve the original physical document even when the final UAE certificate is delivered digitally. [UAE MoFA, Embassy in Islamabad](https://www.mofa.gov.ae/en/Missions/Islamabad/Services/Services-for-Individuals/Attestation-Information).

Before ordering a translation for any destination, ask:

1. Is **Arabic** required or is the English original accepted?
2. Does the organisation need the **whole document**, including reverse-side notes and annotations?
3. Must the translation carry a **translator certification statement**, stamp or signature?
4. Is **notarisation, MOFA authentication or apostille** separately required?
5. Are scanned certified PDFs accepted, or is a signed physical copy required?

If you have an email or checklist from the receiving organisation, send it with the document. It is more useful than assuming all foreign missions accept the same format.

## What does “certified English-to-Arabic translation” mean?

A certified translation is typically supplied with a signed or otherwise authenticated statement describing the translator's or translation provider's responsibility for the translation. **It is not the same as a government-issued approval of the underlying document.** Different organisations can impose their own required form or professional qualifications.

The quotation should clarify the actual deliverables available for your case, which may include:

- An Arabic translation matching the requested document's content.
- A certification statement or translator declaration, where applicable and agreed.
- A signed/stamped copy where that format is offered and accepted by the destination.
- A digital file and/or physical copy based on the agreed order.

**No universal promise:** LISAN.PK does not claim that a translation automatically becomes “Saudi Embassy approved,” “MOFA attested,” or legally valid in every country merely because it bears a translator's stamp.

## Why accurate English-to-Arabic translation matters

A useful translation must help the receiving organisation compare Arabic content with the original English document. Problems that can cause questions include:

- Translating a university name inconsistently across a degree and transcript.
- Mixing an applicant's passport spelling with a different Arabic name spelling without clarification.
- Confusing a **degree**, **provisional certificate** and **transcript**.
- Omitting conditions, marginal notes or reverse-side text the recipient expects to see.
- Changing dates, numeric amounts or contract-party names.
- Mistaking a private translator's certificate for government attestation.
- Applying generic “embassy-approved” language without checking the actual receiving authority.

Quality starts with a complete source and accurate instructions, not an exaggerated acceptance guarantee.
