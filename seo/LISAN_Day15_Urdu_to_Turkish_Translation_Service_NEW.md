## Urdu Nikah Nama to Turkish translation

The Pakistani Nikah Nama is a marriage contract containing legal and personal details that require more than word-by-word substitution. A reliable Turkish translation should accurately reflect the source record, including:

- Spouses' identities and names as shown on supporting documents.
- Marriage date and place, including differences between date formats where relevant.
- Registrar, witnesses and signatories.
- The dower/mehr clauses (for example, immediate or deferred obligations) and any additional contractual terms.
- All official stamps, annotations and handwritten entries that form part of the document.

**Translation is not a marriage registration.** A Turkish translation of an Urdu Nikah Nama does not itself register the marriage with Turkish authorities or replace any required Pakistani MRC. Before commissioning translation, ask whether the receiving office wants the Nikah Nama, MRC, both, or another civil-status document.

## Urdu to Turkish birth certificates and NADRA family records

Pakistani family paperwork is not interchangeable. A **birth certificate** records a birth, **CRC/B-Form** concerns a child's registration, **FRC** summarises family relationships, and **MRC** records marriage registration. The receiving Turkish institution decides which of these best supports its procedure.

For children's records, accurately reproduce each parent's name, registration numbers and dates. Do not invent missing names or silently “correct” discrepancies in the translation. If a name spelling differs across the passport, birth record and FRC, ask the receiving institution what supporting explanation or corrected source record it will accept.

## Urdu to Turkish educational certificate and transcript translation

Pakistani students applying to Turkish universities may need an eligible school certificate, university degree, transcript or entrance-test result translated into Turkish. **Requirements differ significantly across institutions.**

For example:

- **Kayseri University** lists a Turkish translation certified by a sworn translator **and a notary** for certain foreign-student registration records. [1]
- **Boğaziçi University**, in its 2026–27 registration instructions, states that translations from translation agencies are acceptable for specified documents. [2]
- **Ankara University** lists sworn-translator Turkish transcript translations in its foreign-student registration materials. [3]

These are **examples of institution-specific rules**, not one nationwide acceptance guarantee. An Urdu source document translated in Pakistan might still need a translation executed, certified or notarised through a route accepted by the particular Turkish institution.

### How should Pakistani academic terms be handled?

A careful translation distinguishes **Matric / Secondary School Certificate**, **Intermediate / Higher Secondary School Certificate**, grades, academic subjects and degree titles. It should preserve the issuer's terminology and not assert equivalence with a Turkish degree. **Denklik (equivalence)** is a separate decision by an authorised body; a translator cannot award it.

If the institution only accepts English or Turkish records, confirm whether an **official English transcript already issued by your university** is sufficient before paying for another translation.

## Does Turkish law require a sworn translator and notary?

**Sometimes, for particular document types and procedures—but not in every situation.** The official Turkish Directorate of Migration Management describes scenarios in which foreign-language documents require a sworn Turkish translation approved by a notary; universities publish their own registration instructions. [1][2][4]

| Requirement | What it means | Who decides whether it is needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Turkish-language translation | Accurate text in Turkish | Receiving office/university |
| Translator's accuracy statement | Identification and declaration by translator where appropriate | Receiving office's acceptance rules |
| **Yeminli tercüman** | A sworn translator recognised under the applicable Turkish process | Turkish authority/institution; actual translator status must be verified |
| **Noter onayı** | Turkish notarial certification of a document or translation in the applicable route | Receiving authority and notary |
| **Apostille** | Authentication of the origin of an eligible public document | Treaty applicability and destination requirement |
| **Denklik** | Formal academic equivalence decision | Competent education authority—not a translator |

**Before you pay for a “sworn” translation:** ask the receiving office if it accepts a translation produced outside Türkiye, whether the translator must be associated with a Turkish notary, and whether the original record itself must be apostilled. Do not assume a stamp issued by an overseas private agency is identical to the Turkish notarial process.

## Do Pakistani documents for Türkiye need apostille?

Pakistan and Türkiye participate in the Apostille Convention, but **not every translated document must be apostilled**. Applicability depends on whether the record is an eligible public document, treaty relationship, its purpose and the receiving office's specific instructions. An Apostille authenticates the public document's origin; it does **not** validate the translation's accuracy or establish the substantive facts within the document. [5][6]

For a university registration example, distinguish these separate possibilities:

1. Authenticity of the original certificate (issuing board/IBCC or HEC as appropriate).
2. International authentication of the eligible source record, **if demanded**.
3. Turkish-language translation.
4. Sworn-translator and notarial certification, **if demanded**.
5. Academic equivalence, if separately requested.

The order can vary by institution and document. Check the exact intended route rather than assuming “translation first” or “apostille first” is universally correct. Our [Apostille guide](/guides/apostille-pakistan) explains Pakistan's process.

## Urdu-to-Turkish translation for Turkish visa or residence matters

Applicants frequently ask whether their Urdu marriage, birth or education records must be translated for a Turkish visa or residence permit. **Do not rely on a generic requirement**: visa categories, consular checklists, residence-permit subtypes and local authorities have distinct rules.

Start by identifying:

- The exact Turkish visa/residence/registration procedure.
- The official authority receiving your documents.
- The document(s) actually requested.
- Its accepted language, sworn translator and notary requirements.
- Whether the source document also needs verification, apostille or other formalities.

A translation service cannot grant a visa, residence permit, marriage recognition or admission. For current rules consult the [Turkish Presidency of Migration Management](https://en.goc.gov.tr/) or the relevant university and Turkish consular office.

## Common errors to avoid

1. Sending only the first Nikah Nama page when later pages contain contract terms.
2. Confusing a NADRA MRC with the original Urdu Nikah Nama.
3. Ordering Turkish translation before knowing whether the university accepts English.
4. Assuming a Pakistan-based corporate stamp is equivalent to a Turkish **yeminli tercüman** certification.
5. Presenting a translation as proof of academic **denklik**.
6. Apostilling everything without destination instructions.
7. Changing spellings or missing data to “match” a passport rather than flagging the inconsistency.
8. Relying on sample university instructions from a different academic year or institution.

## Frequently asked questions

### 1. Can I translate an Urdu Nikah Nama into Turkish in Pakistan?

Yes, an Urdu-to-Turkish translation can be prepared, but the receiving Turkish authority decides whether it must additionally be sworn, notarised or otherwise authenticated. Confirm requirements before ordering.

### 2. Is Urdu-to-Turkish translation accepted for Turkish university admission?

Acceptance depends on the university and procedure. Kayseri University asks for sworn-and-notarised Turkish translations for certain registration records, while Boğaziçi publishes different rules. [1][2]

### 3. Does a Pakistani translated document automatically count as yeminli tercüme?

No. Turkish sworn-translator status and notarial treatment must be established through the applicable recognised process; an overseas agency's stamp alone is not proof of this status.

### 4. Do I need a notary for Urdu-to-Turkish translation?

Not universally. Some Turkish authorities require it for specified documents, others accept agency or other certified translations. Follow the receiving authority's current rules. [1][2][4]

### 5. Can you translate an Urdu birth certificate or B-Form into Turkish?

These documents can be reviewed for translation. First confirm whether the receiving office asks for a birth certificate, CRC/B-Form, FRC or a different family-status record.

### 6. Do I need both Nikah Nama and MRC translated?

Only if the receiving institution requires both. They are distinct documents; one does not automatically substitute for the other.

### 7. Does a Turkish university require an apostille on a Pakistani degree?

Some institutions require authenticated records; check the university's current list. Do not assume an Apostille is universally required at initial online application.

### 8. Is Turkish translation the same as degree equivalence (denklik)?

No. Translation communicates the source record in Turkish. Formal equivalence is decided separately by the relevant authority.

### 9. Can an Urdu handwritten document be translated?

Often yes, if it is sufficiently legible. Unclear wording may need a better scan or source clarification; translators should not invent illegible details.

### 10. Do you offer urgent translation?

Request a quote with your deadline. Availability and delivery commitments must be confirmed for your actual document and any formalities required.

### 11. How much does an Urdu-to-Turkish Nikah Nama translation cost?

It is quotation-based because handwriting, pages, contract detail and the requested certification route vary. The exact price should be confirmed before ordering.

### 12. Is English-to-Turkish the same service as Urdu-to-Turkish?

No. These are different source languages and should be quoted as separate translation directions. For English-language originals, see our [Turkish translation services](/services/translation/turkish-translation).

### 13. Will Turkish authorities accept my translation with a LISAN.PK seal?

A seal alone cannot guarantee acceptance. Ask the receiving authority what translator qualifications and certification it recognises.

### 14. Do I need to courier original documents to get a quote?

Not ordinarily for an initial quotation. Share a clear digital copy and confirm whether originals are needed for any later, separately authorised procedure.

### 15. What information should I send for a quote?

Share the source document, the destination authority, required translation/certification type, output format and deadline—only through an approved private contact channel.

### 16. Does LISAN.PK issue Turkish notarial certificates?

Not by virtue of being a translation website. Notarial acts belong to the competent notary; any facilitation must be explicitly confirmed as available and legally compliant for the individual request.
