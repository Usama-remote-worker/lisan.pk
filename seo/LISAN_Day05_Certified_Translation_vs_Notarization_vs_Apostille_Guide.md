# Certified Translation vs Notarization vs MOFA Apostille in Pakistan: Which Do You Need in 2026?

**Quick answer:** **Certified translation** explains a document accurately in another language; **notarization** formally witnesses or certifies a signature or document under the applicable legal rules; and a **MOFA apostille** authenticates the origin of an eligible Pakistani **public document** for a country where the Apostille Convention operates with Pakistan. These are **different services**, and one does not automatically replace the others. Your destination, document type and receiving institution determine which combination you need. [HCCH Apostille overview](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/specialised-sections/apostille) · [Pakistan MOFA services](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/).

**Official-source review:** 5 November 2026. LISAN.PK is a private translation provider; we do not issue apostilles or make legalisation decisions for embassies.

## First, the five terms people commonly confuse

**Translation, certification, notarization, attestation and apostille are not interchangeable.** They answer different questions:

| Service | Simple meaning | Main purpose | Typical provider | What it does NOT do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Document translation** | Converts text from the source language into another language | Makes the content readable in the required language | Translator | Does not verify original-document authenticity |
| **Certified translation** | Translation accompanied by a translator's declaration or certification required by the recipient | Attests that the translation is complete/accurate and, where required, states translator competence | Translator / translation company | Does not itself apostille or legalize the original |
| **Notarization** | A notary performs a legally recognised formal act, often related to witnessing or certifying a signature or copy | Satisfies a legal formality where required | Lawfully authorised notary | Does not prove the entire foreign-language text is correctly translated merely because of a notarial stamp |
| **General MOFA attestation / legalization** | Pakistan MOFA authenticates the relevant signature, seal or authority under the ordinary route | Supports documents needing standard legalization rather than apostille | Pakistan MOFA / designated office | Does not replace all required foreign embassy steps |
| **MOFA apostille** | Standard Convention certificate authenticating a qualifying Pakistani public document's origin | Replaces the traditional legalization formality in countries where the Convention operates with Pakistan | Pakistan's designated competent authority | Does not translate the document or validate every statement inside it |

**Example:** A translated Nikah Nama may be complete and accurate yet have **no apostille**. A Pakistani degree may have a valid **apostille** yet still need an English or Arabic translation for the receiving institution. A **notary stamp** on a translator declaration is not proof that an embassy has approved the translation.

## Certified translation: what it actually means

A **certified translation** is normally a complete translation accompanied by a declaration identifying the translator or service and asserting the required level of completeness and accuracy. But **“certified translation” is not one globally standardised document type**. Each institution can specify the language, signing method, declaration, contact information or translator qualifications it accepts.

For example:

- **U.S. immigration:** the rule at [8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-103.2) requires a **full English translation** of foreign-language evidence, plus certification of completeness, accuracy and the translator's competence. This is a **translation requirement**, not a blanket requirement to apostille every USCIS submission.
- **UK visa documents:** [GOV.UK supporting-document guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/visitor-visa-guide-to-supporting-documents/guide-to-supporting-documents-visiting-the-uk) requires specified details where a document needs a translation, including a statement of accuracy, date and translator identification. The exact instruction must be checked for the visa category.
- **Saudi Arabia:** the required language and translator certification can vary by authority, document category and application stage. A Pakistan MOFA apostille does not automatically mean Arabic translation is compulsory.

A reliable translation should preserve personal names, dates, numbers, legal terminology, academic terms, relevant stamps and the source's limitations. If handwriting is illegible, the translator should flag it rather than guess.

### Is a translator's stamp sufficient?

**Only if the receiving institution says so.** A private translation stamp is not automatically a government, notary, embassy or court credential. Some authorities ask for a signed accuracy statement; some require a sworn or formally recognised translator; some request notarization or a special national format. Ask the recipient before ordering extras.

## Notarization: what it does, and what it does not

Notarization is a separate formal legal act. Depending on the jurisdiction and document, it may involve witnessing a signature, verifying a person's identity, certifying a copy or certifying a statement. Its precise legal effect depends on the authorised act and governing law.

A common mistake is paying to notarize a document when the actual requirement is a certified translation—or assuming a notary has checked the linguistic accuracy of a translation. **Unless the notarial act expressly covers the required certification, do not treat the two as equivalent.**

**When might notarization matter?** Examples include a power of attorney, an affidavit, or a receiving authority that explicitly requires the translator's declaration to be notarized. A Pakistani notary also cannot issue a Pakistan MOFA apostille; apostilles are issued by designated **competent authorities** under the Convention. [HCCH overview](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/specialised-sections/apostille) · [Pakistan competent authority information](https://www.hcch.net/en/states/authorities/details3/?aid=1179).

## MOFA attestation versus apostille in Pakistan

MOFA's current public system distinguishes **Apostille Attestation** and **General Attestation**. Both can involve an online application, review and document-specific instructions, but their international use differs. [MOFA portal](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/) · [MOFA general attestation](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/general-attestation) · [MOFA apostille](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/apostille).

| Question | Pakistan MOFA apostille | Pakistan MOFA general attestation |
|---|---|---|
| Where is it normally used? | Eligible public documents for a destination where the Hague Apostille Convention is applicable **with Pakistan** | When the destination or document requires standard legalization rather than the apostille route |
| What does it authenticate? | The origin of the qualifying public document (such as relevant signature/capacity/seal) | The relevant signature, seal or authentication chain under the general service |
| Is a foreign embassy stamp automatically necessary afterwards? | **No**, not as a general legalization layer where the Convention operates | **May be**, depending on destination requirements |
| Is certified translation included? | **No** | **No** |
| Where to check? | [MOFA country directory](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/countries), [HCCH status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41), receiving authority | [MOFA general attestation](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/general-attestation), receiving authority |

An apostille does **not** decide whether a university degree is equivalent to a Saudi qualification, whether a marriage is accepted for a particular visa, or whether a translation is linguistically accurate. It authenticates the official origin of the relevant public document.

### Does every Hague Convention country accept Pakistani apostilles?

**No.** The Convention must operate **between Pakistan and the destination state**. Some contracting states formally objected to Pakistan's accession, meaning the Convention may not operate bilaterally with them. The [HCCH objections recorded against Pakistan's accession](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications/?csid=1466&disp=type) include several European states; this is why checking the **bilateral status**, rather than just two country names on a generic map, matters.

Use **both** the [HCCH status and declarations](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) **and** Pakistan MOFA's [destination directory](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/countries). Then confirm the actual institution's requirements. The same destination may have different rules for public documents, private contracts, professional qualifications and translations.

## Decision tree: which service do I need?

**Start with the receiving institution, not the document-service price list.**

1. **Is the document in a language the recipient accepts?** If **no**, obtain the specific **translation format** it requests. If **yes**, translation may not be needed.
2. **Does the recipient require proof the original public document was officially issued?** If **no**, do not automatically pay for MOFA authentication. If **yes**, continue.
3. **Is it a qualifying Pakistani public document?** Check the source authority. University records may require HEC; board certificates may require IBCC; private documents can need a formal step before eligibility for public-document authentication.
4. **Does the Apostille Convention operate between Pakistan and the destination?** If **yes**, and the document is eligible, check the **MOFA apostille** route. If **no**, check **general attestation and any lawful embassy legalization** required.
5. **Does the receiving authority require notarization or a particular translator status?** If **yes**, identify exactly what act or credential it requires. Do not substitute a generic stamp.
6. **Must the translation itself be authenticated?** If **yes**, ask whether it needs a notarial or other **official certification capable of separate authentication**, rather than assuming an ordinary translation is directly apostillable.
7. **Are there separate admission, professional, court or visa checks?** Complete those only where applicable; an apostille does not replace substantive approval.

**Short version:** **Language requirement → Original-document verification → Destination route (apostille or general legalization) → Recipient-specific translator/notary requirements.** Sequence can change when a recipient requires apostille annotations to be included in the translation.

## Do you translate before or after the apostille?

**There is no one correct sequence for every country.** Decide based on what the receiving authority will inspect.

| Requirement | Practical sequence to confirm |
|---|---|
| Original public document must be apostilled; translation is not required | Source verification, if needed → original apostille → submit |
| Translation must reproduce the apostille certificate and official markings | Source verification → apostille the eligible original → translate original **plus requested annotations** |
| Notarised translator's declaration must itself be authenticated | Confirm approved translator/notary form → obtain required official certification → ask competent authority if that certification is apostillable |
| Admission portal initially requests translated scans only | Certified translation as requested → save further authentication until the university instructs it |
| Destination does not accept a Pakistani apostille under the applicable bilateral treaty | Follow the recipient's confirmed general attestation / legalization procedure; do not assume apostille alone suffices |

**Important:** An apostille on the **original** does not automatically apostille a **separate translation**. The Hague Convention concerns eligible public documents and certain official certifications. Whether a translation-related notarial certificate qualifies is a legal question for the competent authority. [HCCH](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/specialised-sections/apostille) · [MOFA checklist](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/apostille-checklist).

## Real-world document examples: Pakistan to other countries

These are **illustrative decision paths**, not claims that every person needs every step.

### Example 1: Pakistani BS degree for a Saudi university or employer

- Identify the university/employer's exact instructions and whether authentication is needed **now or later**.
- Check the applicable HEC degree-verification step.
- If a Pakistan MOFA apostille is required, use MOFA's official online route. Saudi Arabia is listed in MOFA's directory as accepting apostilles.
- Obtain Arabic or English translation only as requested.
- Check any **additional academic or professional approval** separately. Saudi MOFA's published ratification conditions continue to refer to Saudi Cultural Attaché and embassy steps for some foreign university records, so do not promise this step is universally required or universally obsolete.

### Example 2: Urdu Nikah Nama for a UK visa application

The receiving UK visa route may require a properly **certified English translation** of a foreign-language record. It does not follow that every UK visa submission also needs a Pakistani apostille. Confirm the case-specific document checklist and translation requirements. [GOV.UK document guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/visitor-visa-guide-to-supporting-documents/guide-to-supporting-documents-visiting-the-uk).

Read [Nikah Nama and MRC Translation Services](/services/translation/nikah-nama-marriage-certificate) if you need to distinguish the original Urdu marriage contract from the computerized MRC.

### Example 3: Urdu birth record for a U.S. immigration filing

The U.S. immigration translation rule at [8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/section-103.2) requires a complete English translation with the specified certification for foreign-language evidence. It does **not** impose a blanket apostille requirement on every translated birth certificate submitted to USCIS. Follow the particular form instructions and evidence requirements.

### Example 4: Pakistani school certificate for an overseas application

A school or board certificate may first need verification by the issuing board and [IBCC](https://ibcc.edu.pk/attestation/) where appropriate. The applicant then checks whether the destination accepts a Pakistani apostille and whether the school, university or visa office wants a translated version. **HEC is not the default authority for Matric or Intermediate board certificates.**

### Example 5: Power of attorney for use abroad

A power of attorney can have special identity, signature, notarial and personal-appearance requirements. Determine whether the recipient wants a Pakistani notarial act, Pakistan MOFA authentication, a Convention apostille, or consular legalization. Do not treat ordinary document translation as sufficient for a legal instrument. Follow MOFA's [document-specific SOPs](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/apostille-checklist).

### Example 6: Pakistan-issued document for a country that objected to Pakistan's accession

Do not assume that country accepts a Pakistani apostille merely because both countries appear on the HCCH party list. Check the [HCCH objection notifications](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications/?csid=1466&disp=type), Pakistan MOFA's [destination page](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/countries) and the receiving authority's current legalization route. A different authentication chain may be needed.

| **HEC** | University degrees, transcripts and eligible higher-education records | Presenting an unrelated high-school record as a university degree |

## Can you apostille a certified translation in Pakistan?

**Do not assume that any private translation can be apostilled directly.** An apostille authenticates eligible public documents, which can include certain **official certifications** under the Convention. A translator's private statement, a notarial certificate relating to the translator's signature, and the original civil record are legally different items.

Before ordering a translation-related apostille, ask two specific questions:

1. **Receiving authority:** “Do you need an apostille on the **original document**, a **certified translation**, or a separate official/notarised certification of the translation?”
2. **Pakistan MOFA / competent authority:** “Is the exact official certification attached to this translation **eligible** for an apostille, and what prior verification is required?”

If the answers are unclear, do not pay for repeated notarization, embassy stamps or duplicate apostilles. A notarised translation does not automatically become universally acceptable abroad.

## What details should you send to request a certified translation?

A useful translation enquiry includes the following:

1. A **complete and readable scan** of the document, including all relevant pages, seals and reverse-side markings.
2. The **original language and required target language** (for example Urdu → Arabic or Urdu → English).
3. The **destination country and receiving organisation** (university, visa authority, employer or court).
4. The exact **translation certification wording**, if supplied in the recipient's checklist.
5. Whether the **original already carries HEC, IBCC, MOFA or apostille markings** that must be translated.
6. The required delivery format: electronic PDF, signed hard copy, notarised declaration if specifically required, and any deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

### 1. Is certified translation the same as apostille?

**No.** Translation converts content into another language; an apostille authenticates the origin of an eligible public document under the Convention. You may need either, both or neither, depending on the recipient.

### 2. Is MOFA attestation the same as translation?

No. MOFA verifies the relevant public-document authentication, while a translator handles language conversion and any requested accuracy statement.

### 3. Is notarization compulsory for certified translation in Pakistan?

**Not universally.** It depends on the requirements of the receiving authority and the legal form of the document or translation declaration.

### 4. Can a notary issue an apostille?

No. Apostilles are issued by designated competent authorities. A notary may perform a separate formal act that can, in some cases, be relevant to later authentication.

### 5. Is apostille accepted in Saudi Arabia for Pakistani documents?

Saudi Arabia is listed as accepting apostilles in the Pakistan MOFA directory, subject to document eligibility and any separate receiving-authority requirements. See our [Saudi-specific guide](/blog/saudi-embassy-attested-document-translation-process-pakistan).

### 6. Does every Apostille Convention country accept Pakistani apostilles?

No. Check bilateral objections to Pakistan's accession and the receiving authority's requirements. Being listed as a Convention party does not by itself establish that the Convention operates between every two parties.

### 7. Is an apostille a guarantee that my degree is genuine or equivalent?

No. It authenticates the relevant official signature/capacity/seal; it does not guarantee the truth of every claim in the degree or its recognition by a foreign institution.

### 8. Should I translate first and apostille later?

There is no universal order. If the recipient wants apostille text included in the translation, it can be practical to apostille the eligible original first. If it wants a separately authenticated translator declaration, the sequence may differ.

### 9. Can I apostille my Urdu-to-English translation separately?

Only if the **specific public certification associated with that translation** is eligible and the receiving authority requires it. Ask the competent authority before ordering a notarization or apostille.

### 10. Do UK visas require a Pakistani apostille for every translated document?

No blanket rule can be inferred from the general UKVI translation requirement. Check the relevant immigration category and document checklist.

### 11. Does USCIS require translation and an apostille together?

The general USCIS translation rule concerns a complete, certified English translation of foreign-language evidence. It does not make an apostille universally compulsory for every filing.

### 12. Are HEC and IBCC both required for every educational document?

No. They deal with different classes of educational records. Check the issuing institution and the relevant authority.

### 13. Does MOFA online registration mean I never need to visit an office?

No. MOFA requires advance registration but its published procedure continues to describe appointments or physical handover for certain applications.

### 14. Does apostille certify the correctness of the translation?

No. An apostille authenticates the eligible public document or official certification to which it relates; language accuracy is a separate matter.

### 15. Does the translator have to be “embassy approved”?

Only if a **specific current recipient requirement** actually requires a recognised or registered translator. Do not assume a private stamp is official accreditation, or that every receiving authority uses the same rules.

### 16. Is there one universal price for “translation plus MOFA apostille”?

No. Government fees depend on document type, while translation, courier and any lawful additional services are separate. Check [MOFA's published fees](https://attestation.mofa.gov.pk/fees) and request an itemised translation quotation.

## Final decision: what should you order first?

**First ask the recipient what it needs; then order only those services.** If it needs English or Arabic text, obtain a properly certified translation. If it needs the origin of a Pakistani public document authenticated for a destination where the Convention applies with Pakistan, check the MOFA apostille route. If it asks for notarization, identify the exact notarial act. Where a conventional legalization route is required instead, follow the competent authority's current directions.

Do not confuse **more stamps** with **greater acceptance**. The correct document, the right recipient-specific format and an accurate translation matter more than unnecessary extra processing.
