# USCIS Certified Translation Requirements: Urdu and Arabic Documents into English

**USCIS generally requires a full English translation of any foreign-language information in a document submitted for an immigration benefit.** A translator must sign a statement confirming that the translation is **complete and accurate** and that the translator is **competent** to translate into English. The current Form I-130 instructions also call for the translator's signature, printed name, signature date and contact information. [Source: USCIS Form I-130 Instructions.]

## What is a USCIS-certified English translation?

It is an English translation accompanied by a signed declaration from the translator explaining the translation's completeness and accuracy and the translator's competence. “Certified” describes the **translator's signed attestation**; it does not mean USCIS has pre-approved the translation company or given a government certification to the translator.

The rule is found at **8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)**. For a spouse petition, the current **I-130 instructions** specify the translator's printed name, signature, date and contact information. A filing may involve additional instructions; check the current instructions for your particular USCIS form. [1, 2]

### At a glance: what to submit

| Element | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Complete source document | A readable copy of all relevant pages and reverse-side entries | The translation must correspond to the document submitted |
| Full English translation | All meaningful foreign-language information is rendered in English | An extract or summary may not satisfy the instruction for a *full* translation |
| Accuracy certification | Translator confirms completeness and accuracy | A translation without a clear declaration may not meet the filing instruction |
| Competence statement | Translator confirms ability to translate the relevant language into English | The certifier must genuinely be competent in that language pair |
| Signature and details | Signed, dated certification with the translator's printed name and contact information | Included in current I-130 instructions |
| Relevant filing instructions | Correct form and any case-specific USCIS notice | One service page cannot replace requirements for every immigration case |

### Nikah Nama translation for Form I-130 and immigrant spouse visas

A Pakistani **Nikah Nama** and a government-issued **MRC** are different records. The U.S. Department of State's Pakistan reciprocity schedule names the **MRC** as the marriage certificate; it describes authorized local issuing authorities. For USCIS, any Urdu text submitted as evidence requires a complete certified English translation. For NVC and U.S. Embassy Islamabad, separate civil-document and interview instructions also apply. ## Arabic-to-English certified translation for USCIS

Applicants who have lived or worked in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar or another Arabic-speaking country may need English translations of Arabic-language records when submitted with a USCIS filing. Possible examples include:

- Arabic marriage or birth certificates.
- Arabic divorce or court documents.
- Saudi employment or administrative records, where relevant.
- Arabic education or professional documents, where a USCIS filing asks for them.

Arabic documents often contain transliterated names, Hijri or Gregorian dates and government abbreviations. A translator should reproduce the source meaning faithfully and identify unreadable entries; translation is not a method for altering an official identity record.

## Example USCIS translator certification statement

This is a **generic example**, not an executed certificate and not a claim that one exact text is prescribed for every USCIS form:

> **CERTIFICATE OF TRANSLATION ACCURACY**  
> I, **[full printed name]**, certify that I am competent to translate from **[Urdu or Arabic] into English**, and that the attached English translation of **[document description]** is complete and accurate.  
> Translator's printed name: **[name]**  
> Signature: **[actual signature]**  
> Date signed: **[date]**  
> Contact details: **[valid contact information]**

Only the person who actually translated and certifies the document should complete and sign the declaration. A stamp or logo by itself does not substitute for the statement of competence and accuracy.

### Does USCIS require a translator's U.S. license?

**The general translation rule does not identify a universal USCIS licensing program for translators.** The regulation and I-130 instructions describe translation completeness and certification of competence. A company should not market itself as “USCIS-approved” or “U.S. Embassy-approved” merely because it offers this service. The receiving authority evaluates what was submitted. [1, 2]

### Must the translator be independent?

The governing translation clause does not state that every translation must be prepared by a particular commercial company or that all translations prepared by relatives are automatically prohibited. Competence and accurate certification remain essential. To minimise disputes, many applicants prefer a professional translator who is independent of the case. Check the relevant form instructions and obtain immigration advice when impartiality or a conflict of interest is in question.

### Is notarisation required for a USCIS translation?

**Not generally under 8 CFR 103.2(b)(3).** The general rule requires a translator's signed certification. Notarisation and apostille are different services; they should not be added to a translation quote as automatic immigration requirements. If an official notice specifically requests additional authentication, follow that request. [1]

## USCIS vs NVC: translation requirements are not always the same

USCIS handles immigration petitions, while the U.S. Department of State and NVC handle civil-document review for applicable immigrant-visa processing. NVC's general instructions allow an exception for a document already in the official language of the visa-interview country. However, the U.S. Embassy Islamabad's interview checklist lists an **English translation of the marriage certificate** for married applicants. [3–5]

| Filing stage | Main thing to verify |
|---|---|
| **USCIS petition** | Full English translation of any foreign-language evidence submitted, with translator certification |
| **NVC / CEAC** | Correct civil document under the reciprocity schedule; NVC language rule and document slot |
| **Interview in Islamabad** | Embassy's original-document, English translation and photocopy instructions |

**Do not confuse a successful CEAC upload with USCIS acceptance or a final visa decision.** They are separate stages.

## Common mistakes in USCIS translation requests

**Sending only a summary:** The filing instruction requires a *full* English translation where foreign-language material is submitted.

**Claiming an official record was corrected by translation:** A translated name variation does not change what appears on the source document.

**Missing certification information:** An unsigned statement or one that omits the translator's competence and accuracy declaration may not match the relevant form instructions.

**Assuming stamps or apostilles equal certified translations:** These processes do different things.

**Treating every NVC upload like a USCIS petition:** Review the correct government requirements for the authority and stage involved.

**Sharing unnecessary personal documents:** A quote normally begins with the relevant document only; only request extra details if required for assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

### What qualifies as certified translation for USCIS?

A full English translation of a submitted foreign-language document, with a signed translator statement attesting completeness, accuracy and competence, meeting the current instructions for the particular filing. [1, 2]

### Do I need an Urdu-to-English Nikah Nama translation for I-130?

If you submit an Urdu Nikah Nama or other Urdu marriage evidence to USCIS, the foreign-language text must be fully translated into English and certified according to the filing instructions. Consult the Pakistan reciprocity information for the correct official marriage certificate. [2–4]

### Does USCIS require both Nikah Nama and MRC?

The Department of State identifies the MRC as Pakistan's marriage certificate. Specific USCIS evidence requests can vary. Review the actual I-130 instruction and any case-specific notice; do not rely on an invented universal three-document rule. [2, 3]

### Can a bilingual MRC be submitted without a separate English translation?

Review whether all relevant foreign-language information appears fully in English and what the receiving authority requests. Bilingual printing alone does not always answer this question.

### Does a translation have to retain the Nikah Nama's original layout?

A consistent field-by-field layout is useful, but the central USCIS rule is a full English translation with proper certification; it is not a requirement to reproduce the original design pixel by pixel. [1, 2]

### Can I use an Arabic-to-Urdu translation for USCIS?

No. If a USCIS filing needs a translation of an Arabic document, the required destination language is **English**, not Urdu. [1]

### Does the translator need to sign and date the certificate?

Yes. The current Form I-130 instructions require a signature, printed name, signature date and contact information, alongside the accuracy and competence statements. [2]

### Is a stamp mandatory for USCIS certification?

The relevant translation instruction emphasizes the signed certification, not possession of a particular agency seal. A company stamp may appear as part of an agency's normal paperwork but does not replace the required signed declaration. [1, 2]

### Do you guarantee approval or no Request for Evidence?

No. Translation quality matters, but USCIS decides document sufficiency and benefit eligibility. No translator can guarantee a government outcome.
