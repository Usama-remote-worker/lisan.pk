## Which Arabic documents can be translated into English?

## Saudi Iqama Arabic-to-English translation

The Saudi **Iqama** is a residence identification document. Applicants sometimes need English rendering of Arabic entries for a bank, overseas employer, immigration file or other administrative request. A translation is **not** a replacement for the original Iqama or independent proof of immigration status.

A good Arabic→English Iqama translation should preserve:

- The holder's full Arabic name as written, without silently altering uncertain spellings.
- Any Latin name already printed on the card, for comparison.
- Iqama/residence number and other identifiers exactly as visible.
- Dates and their original calendar labels, where present.
- Occupation, employer or sponsor information when it appears.
- Notes, restrictions, reverse-side text, stamps and additional fields.

### What if the English spelling differs from my passport?

Tell us **before translation** and provide the preferred passport spelling **only when necessary for consistent identity references**. The translated document must not falsify the Arabic source; meaningful differences should be handled transparently, for example with a translator's note if appropriate and accepted by the recipient.

## Arabic-to-English translation for visa and immigration files

Requirements differ between **UKVI**, **USCIS** and other receiving bodies. A translation that meets one authority's rules is **not automatically certified for all authorities worldwide**.

### United Kingdom: documents not in English or Welsh

GOV.UK's [visitor supporting-document guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/visitor-visa-guide-to-supporting-documents/guide-to-supporting-documents-visiting-the-uk) says documents not in English or Welsh must be accompanied by a full translation that can be independently verified. Guidance identifies details such as a statement that the translation is accurate, the translation date and the translator's or company official's identification and contact information. **Check your specific visa category**, because its evidence requirements may differ.

For UK use, tell us:

1. The visa category and receiving organisation.
2. Whether a full certified translation is specified.
3. Whether the authority has asked for any particular certification or additional originals.

### United States: USCIS foreign-language document translations

Under [8 CFR §103.2(b)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-103/subpart-A/section-103.2), a foreign-language document submitted to USCIS must be accompanied by a **full English translation** certified as complete and accurate, together with certification that the translator is competent to translate from the source language into English. This is a **translation requirement**, not an automatic demand for apostille or notary stamping on every USCIS filing.

If you require a translation for USCIS, provide the complete Arabic document, including any back page or stamp, and tell us the form or case type. We can review the requested certification format before preparing a quotation.

### Canada, Australia, universities and private employers

There is no single certification statement that can be assumed sufficient for every receiving authority. Some institutions specify translator accreditation, sworn-translation requirements, format or certified copies. **Ask the receiving office for its current checklist first.**

## Degree, transcript and academic-record translation from Arabic to English

Arabic academic documents may include abbreviations, non-Latin institution names, Hijri dates, Arabic numerals or local grading categories. A faithful translation should retain the source meaning and not claim unverified educational equivalence.

We can assess quotations for documents such as:

- University degrees and diplomas issued in Arabic.
- Transcripts with Arabic course names and grading labels.
- School certificates and training records.
- Academic letters, enrolment confirmations and employment-training certificates.

**Translation is not credential evaluation.** Only the appropriate recognition or evaluation body can determine the qualification's academic or professional equivalence, if required.

## Arabic legal contracts and official records: accuracy before speed

Legal Arabic often contains formulaic expressions, nested obligations and defined terms. A **literal word-for-word rendering** can distort an obligation, while an overly free summary may omit legally relevant clauses. The translation should preserve the document's actual substance, including:

- Parties, identification numbers and addresses.
- Dates, monetary amounts, contract periods and payment terms.
- Conditions, exceptions, prohibitions and termination clauses.
- Annexes, footnotes, stamps, handwritten changes and signatures.
- Terminology whose English equivalent depends on the document's jurisdiction.

A translator can clarify ambiguous source wording but must not act as a lawyer or silently repair a defective document. For legal decisions, obtain advice from a qualified legal professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## What does “certified Arabic-to-English translation” mean?

A **certified translation** normally combines the translated text with a signed certification or declaration identifying the translator or translation provider and attesting to accuracy and completeness **to the standard required by the receiving organisation**. Certification is not identical to notarisation, apostille or consular legalisation.

| Document component | What it does | Does it replace translation? |
|---|---|---|
| Arabic original | Shows the issued source document | No |
| English translation | Renders the source content in English | This is the actual translation |
| Translator's certification statement | States accuracy/competence in a verifiable format | It accompanies, rather than replaces, the translation |
| Notarial acknowledgment (if requested) | Addresses specified signature or notarial formalities | No |
| Apostille / government legalisation (if required) | Authenticates specified official signature/seal through the applicable route | No |

**We do not claim blanket “embassy approved”, “100% acceptance” or government-issued translator licensing.** The receiving authority determines whether the provided translation and supporting documents satisfy its instructions.
